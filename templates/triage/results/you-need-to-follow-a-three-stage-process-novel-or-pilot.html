You need to follow the 3-stage business case process.

Based on your answers, your proposal should follow the 3-stage business case process.

The template you need will depend on the stage your proposal has reached. This may be a:

  • Project Brief
  • Strategic Outline Case (SOC)
  • Outline Business Case (OBC)
  • Full Business Case (FBC)

In some cases, such as a change to an approved business case, you may not need a new template. Instead, you may be able to update the existing business case using an addendum.

Always use the latest template from the template library. Avoid using a template from a previous business case or one shared by a colleague.

! Warning You do not need to return to this service to upload your business case after it has been approved.

What to do next

If you know which template you need

  • Speak to your Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) as early as possible.
  • Download the template from the template library

If you're not sure which template you need

  • Speak to your Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) as early as possible.
  • Review the guidance on the Project Delivery Hub to identify the stage your proposal has reached.
  • If you're still unsure, contact the Investment Sub-Committee (ISC) Secretariat. They can help you identify the correct starting template and explain the approval process.

What approvals can I expect?

The approvals required for your proposal depend on:

  • the value and type of funding involved
  • whether this is a new proposal or a change to an existing one
  • whether approval authority has already been delegated
Help with acronyms

RDEL
Resource Departmental Expenditure Limits
Funding used for day-to-day running costs, resources and administration.

CDEL
Capital Departmental Expenditure Limit
Capital Departmental Expenditure Limits (money that is spent on investment and things that will create growth in the future)

CDEL FT
Capital Departmental Expenditure Limit Financial Transactions
Funding used for investments that create or improve assets.

RDEL Admin
Resource Departmental Expenditure Limit for Administration
Capital funding that is expected to be repaid, such as loans or investments.

Local Gov DEL
Local Government Departmental Expenditure Limit
Funding provided to local authorities.

S31 grant
Funding provided to local authorities under Section 31 of the Local Government Act 2003.
